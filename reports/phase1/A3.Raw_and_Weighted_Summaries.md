# A3. Comparison of Unweighted and Weighted Summaries

To illustrate the effect of weighting, the unweighted and weighted prevalence of any dietary supplement use were compared among adults aged 20 years or older with a positive `WTDRD1` weight and a valid yes/no response to `DSD010`.

| Summary | Unweighted estimate | `WTDRD1`-weighted estimate |
|---|---:|---:|
| Adults reporting any dietary supplement use (`DSD010 = 1`) | 64.7% (3,090 of 4,775) | 60.2% |

The weighted estimate is 4.5 percentage points lower than the unweighted estimate. This shows that the sample proportion of supplement users differs from the population estimate after accounting for the NHANES dietary sample weights. The weights account for unequal selection probabilities, additional nonresponse, and the weekday/weekend allocation of dietary interviews.

As a project-related comparison, mean sleep duration was also summarized by supplement-use group. Only sleep-duration values from 3 to 13.5 hours were used for these means because NHANES codes the lower and upper values as grouped categories (`2` means less than 3 hours; `14` means 14 hours or more), rather than exact sleep durations.

| Sleep measure and group | Unweighted mean | `WTDRD1`-weighted mean | Valid participants |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Weekday/workday sleep — supplement use reported | 7.76 hours | 7.71 hours | 3,051 |
| Weekday/workday sleep — no supplement use reported | 7.61 hours | 7.60 hours | 1,648 |
| Weekend/non-workday sleep — supplement use reported | 8.23 hours | 8.29 hours | 3,047 |
| Weekend/non-workday sleep — no supplement use reported | 8.23 hours | 8.29 hours | 1,643 |

For weekday/workday sleep, the unweighted difference between the two groups was about 0.15 hours, compared with about 0.11 hours after weighting. For weekend/non-workday sleep, the weighted group means were nearly identical. These results are descriptive point estimates. They do not establish that the groups differ statistically, and they do not imply a causal effect of supplement use on sleep.

The unweighted summaries describe the participants in the analyzed sample. The weighted summaries account for the NHANES dietary sample weights and are more appropriate for population-level interpretation. This comparison is an introductory demonstration of the effect of weighting; it does not report design-based standard errors or confidence intervals.

The weighted supplement-use proportion was calculated as the sum of `WTDRD1` among participants reporting supplement use divided by the sum of `WTDRD1` among participants with a valid yes/no response. Weighted sleep means were calculated within each supplement-use group as the weighted sum of sleep hours divided by the sum of `WTDRD1` for participants with valid sleep hours in that group.

Sources: NHANES recommends `WTDRD1` for analyses of 30-day dietary supplement use and explains that it accounts for additional nonresponse and weekday/weekend allocation of dietary interviews ([`DSQTOT_L` analytic notes](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2021/DataFiles/DSQTOT_L.htm)). NHANES also describes the cycle’s sample design and guidance on selecting weights for analyses that combine components ([2021–2023 analytic guidelines](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/continuousnhanes/overviewbrief.aspx?Cycle=2021-2023)).
