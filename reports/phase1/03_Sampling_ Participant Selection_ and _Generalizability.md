## 3. Sampling, Participant Selection, and Generalizability

NHANES does not use a simple random sample of all U.S. residents. It uses a complex, multistage probability-sampling design to select people from the U.S. civilian, noninstitutionalized population. In general, geographic areas are selected first, followed by smaller area segments, dwelling units, and then eligible people within households. This design gives people different probabilities of selection and is intended to support estimates for the target population when the appropriate survey weights and design information are used.

The August 2021–August 2023 cycle used an updated sample design following the interruption of NHANES field operations during the COVID-19 pandemic. In this cycle, NHANES did not oversample participants by race and Hispanic origin or income. Selection within households also varied by age: all encountered household members aged 0–19 years and 60 years or older were selected, while the number selected among adults aged 20–59 depended on the household size. These design features mean that the sample composition and precision for some subgroups may differ from those in earlier NHANES cycles.

NHANES is observational: participants were not randomly assigned to use dietary supplements. For this project, the planned analysis focuses on adults aged 20 years or older who have the information needed to examine reported supplement use and sleep duration. Although the sleep questionnaire was available to participants aged 16 years or older, the project’s adult-age restriction narrows the population represented by the analysis.

The NHANES design is intended to support generalization to the U.S. civilian, noninstitutionalized population, but results from this project can be generalized only with appropriate consideration of the survey design, weights, participation, and missing data. The final analytic sample will also be limited to people who meet the project’s eligibility rules and have the required sleep and supplement information. Therefore, the final conclusions will apply most directly to eligible adults represented by the analyzed data, rather than automatically to every U.S. resident or to people outside the NHANES target population. The exact number of people retained at each eligibility and data-availability step will be reported when the analytic population is finalized.

Because supplement use was not assigned experimentally and the data are cross-sectional, any observed difference or association between supplement use and sleep should be interpreted as an association, not as evidence that supplement use caused a change in sleep.
