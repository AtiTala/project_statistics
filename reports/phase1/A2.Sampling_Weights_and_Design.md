# A2. NHANES Sample Weights and Design Variables

## Selected sample weight

The primary sample weight for this project is `WTDRD1`, the dietary day one sample weight. The main exposure is 30-day dietary supplement use from `DSQTOT_L`. NHANES documentation specifically instructs analysts to use `WTDRD1` when analyzing the 30-day dietary supplement data.

In the August 2021–August 2023 cycle, the supplement-use interview was conducted after the first 24-hour dietary recall. `WTDRD1` starts from the Mobile Examination Center sample weight and includes further adjustments for additional nonresponse and for the different allocation of dietary interviews across weekdays and weekends. It is therefore the appropriate weight for the supplement-use component of this analysis.

The sleep measures are from the `SLQ_L` questionnaire, which does not provide a separate sleep-specific sample weight. Because the analysis combines sleep information with the 30-day supplement-use component, the analysis will use the weight specified by NHANES for the supplement data, `WTDRD1`. The analytic dataset must be restricted to participants with a positive `WTDRD1` and valid values for the variables included in each analysis.

## Survey design variables

The design variables are:

- `SDMVSTRA`: masked variance pseudo-stratum
- `SDMVPSU`: masked variance pseudo-primary sampling unit

These variables are provided in `DEMO_L` and are linked to the participant-level analysis dataset using `SEQN`. The sample weight is used when calculating population estimates. The strata and PSU variables are used with the weight to account for the NHANES sampling design when estimating uncertainty, such as standard errors and confidence intervals.

## Why weighting is needed

NHANES uses a stratified, clustered, multistage probability sample rather than a simple random sample. Participants can have different probabilities of selection and different response patterns. A sample weight represents the number of people in the target population represented by a sampled participant, after the relevant selection and nonresponse adjustments. Accordingly, weighted estimates are needed for population-level interpretation, while the strata and PSU variables are needed to account for the design when estimating uncertainty.

## Preliminary weight-availability check

The downloaded `DSQTOT_L` file contains 6,754 positive `WTDRD1` values. The codebook identifies a value of zero for participants whose Day 1 dietary recall was not done or was incomplete. In the merged adult dataset of 6,064 participants, 4,798 have a positive `WTDRD1`; 4,775 of those also have a valid yes/no response for `DSD010`. These are preliminary availability counts, not the final sample size for either sleep analysis. The final number will depend on the sleep outcome being analyzed and the project’s eligibility and missing-data rules.

NHANES advises analysts to use the appropriate component weight and design information and notes that weight choice should reflect the most restrictive data component used in a combined analysis. `WTDRD1` is selected here because the main supplement-use exposure comes from the dietary supplement component and the cycle-specific `DSQTOT_L` documentation explicitly identifies this weight for 30-day supplement analyses.
