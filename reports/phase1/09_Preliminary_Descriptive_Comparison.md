## 9. Preliminary Descriptive Comparison

Usual sleep duration was compared between adults who reported dietary supplement use in the past 30 days and adults who reported no use. The comparison is unweighted and descriptive. It includes adults with a valid yes/no response for `DSD010`, regardless of whether `WTDRD1` is positive. Thus, the weekday comparison includes 4,702 participants and the weekend comparison includes 4,693. A4 reports the slightly smaller positive-weight analytic samples (4,699 and 4,690, respectively), which are used for the weighted summaries in A3. It uses reported sleep values from 3 to 13.5 hours; missing sleep values and the NHANES grouped endpoint categories (“less than 3 hours” and “14 hours or more”) are excluded because the endpoint codes do not represent exact durations.

| Sleep variable | Supplement use reported: n | Median (Q1, Q3) | No supplement use reported: n | Median (Q1, Q3) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Weekday/workday sleep, hours | 3,052 | 8.0 (7.0, 8.5) | 1,650 | 8.0 (7.0, 8.5) |
| Weekend/non-workday sleep, hours | 3,048 | 8.0 (7.5, 9.0) | 1,645 | 8.0 (7.0, 9.0) |

The weekday sleep medians and quartiles are the same in both groups. For weekend sleep, the median is also 8 hours in both groups; the lower quartile is slightly higher among participants reporting supplement use, while the upper quartile is the same. Overall, the descriptive summaries and boxplots show substantial overlap between the groups and no clear separation in their central sleep durations.

This comparison does not establish that the groups differ in the population, and it does not show that supplement use causes a change in sleep. The exposure is also broad: it indicates any reported supplement use, rather than use of a specific supplement or ingredient. The comparison should therefore be interpreted as an initial description of the current sample, not as a conclusion about a particular supplement’s relationship with sleep.

Source for the grouped sleep-duration codes: [NHANES `SLQ_L` documentation](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2021/DataFiles/SLQ_L.htm).
